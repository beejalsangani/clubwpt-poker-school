Table Selection

The best players don't just play well. They pick where to play.

Two tables, same stakes, same game. One is full of players who fold to every bet. The other has six regulars who've each played a million hands.

The ClubWPT lobby gives you four numbers on every table card: Players, Wait, Avg Pot, and Hands/Hr. Those four tell you most of what you need before you sit.

Your edge at those two tables is completely different, and choosing between them takes ten seconds.

Table selection is the least glamorous skill in poker and one of the most valuable. It requires no study and no practice. It just requires looking before you sit.

What to look for in the lobby

Average pot size. The single most useful number available to you. Large average pots mean loose, action-heavy play — more players seeing flops, more money changing hands. Small average pots mean tight, cautious play where pots get won pre-flop and there's little to fight over.

Players seated. Shown as a fraction, like 6/9. A full table plays slower and tighter. A short-handed table plays fast and aggressive. An empty one isn't a game at all — skip it.

Wait list. A table with people waiting is usually a good game. Players don't queue for a table nobody wants to sit at.

Hands per hour. Faster tables mean more decisions, which means your edge, or your leak, compounds quicker.

Once you're seated

The lobby gives you a snapshot. The table gives you the truth. Spend your first few orbits watching rather than playing:

Who's playing too many hands? They'll show down weak holdings and call bets they shouldn't. This is the player you want on your right, where you act after them.

Who folds to every bet? Predictable and exploitable in a different way.

Who's raising constantly? Note where they're sitting. You want them on your right, not your left.

Is anyone clearly better than the rest? If two or three players are obviously strong, your edge at that table is small. There's another table.

The part nobody does

Leave a bad table. Most players sit down and stay because they sat down. If the table has tightened up, if the loose player left, if you've got two strong regulars on your left — go. There's no cost. It's a ring game.

Changing tables is the cheapest strategic adjustment in poker and almost nobody uses it.
