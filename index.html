How Much to Bring to the Table

Your stack size decides which hands are worth playing.

Sit down at a ring game and the first thing you choose is how many chips to bring. Most players give this about two seconds of thought. It deserves more, because your stack size changes which hands make money.

Think in big blinds, not chips

Chip amounts mean nothing on their own. What matters is your stack measured in big blinds. A player with 100 big blinds is playing the same game whether the blinds are tiny or enormous.

The standard is 100 big blinds. Most strategy you'll read assumes it, most regulars sit with it, and it's a reasonable default until you have a specific reason to do something else.

Why deeper stacks change the math

Here's the concept that makes stack size matter: implied odds. It's the money you expect to win on later streets when you hit your hand, not just the money in the pot right now.

Small pairs are the clearest example. Pocket threes will flop a set roughly one time in eight. That's a losing proposition if there's nothing to win when it happens. But when everyone at the table has 200 big blinds, hitting that set can win an enormous pot. The seven times you miss cost you one small bet each. The one time you hit pays for all of them.

Now shorten the stacks to 30 big blinds. Same pocket threes, same one-in-eight. But the most you can win is 30 blinds, and that no longer covers the misses. The hand goes from profitable to unprofitable, and nothing about the cards changed.

The principle: deep stacks favor hands that can make huge hands — small pairs, suited connectors, anything that flops big or folds. Short stacks favor hands that are simply strong right now — big pairs, big aces.

Practical guidance

Sitting with 100 big blinds is right for almost everyone almost always. Play it until you have a reason not to.

Sitting short, 40 to 50 big blinds, simplifies your decisions. Fewer post-flop choices, less room for expensive mistakes. Some players use this deliberately while learning.

Sitting deep, 200+ big blinds, rewards post-flop skill and punishes its absence. The pots get big and the mistakes get expensive. Get comfortable at 100 first.

Reload when you drop. If you're playing a strategy built for 100 big blinds and you're sitting with 40, you're playing the wrong strategy for your stack. Either top up or adjust.
