Position: The Most Underrated Edge in Poker

Acting last is worth more than most of the hands you're waiting for.

If you only take one thing from this course, take this one.

Position means where you sit relative to the dealer button, which decides when you act. Act after your opponents and you're in position. Act before them and you're out of position.

That's the whole concept. Here's why it's worth more than it sounds.

Information is the entire game

Poker is a game of incomplete information. Everything you do is an estimate based on what you know. Position determines how much you know when you have to decide.

In position, you watch everyone act before you commit. Someone bets big, someone folds fast, someone checks with obvious reluctance. You've collected all of it before spending a chip.

Out of position, you act into darkness. You bet, and then you find out what everyone else thinks. Every decision you make is a guess that they get to respond to with full information.

Same cards. Different game.

What position lets you do

Control the size of the pot. In position, you decide whether a pot stays small or grows. Checking behind keeps it cheap with a marginal hand. Betting builds it when you're strong. Out of position, your opponent makes that choice for you.

Bluff more effectively. When your opponent checks to you twice, they're telling you something. Acting last lets you hear it before deciding whether to represent a hand.

Get more value. In position, you can size your bets against the information you've already gathered instead of guessing.

Fold more cheaply. You'll put fewer chips into pots you were going to lose, because you find out sooner.

How to actually use this

Play more hands in position, fewer out of position. This is the single highest-return adjustment available to most players. A hand that's a clear fold from early position can be a comfortable call on the button. Same hand, same table, different seat.

Be selective from the blinds. You're out of position for the entire hand after the flop, every time. That's a permanent disadvantage and it's why the blinds are the toughest seats at the table.

Notice who's on your left. The aggressive player to your left will act after you all night. That's a real cost. If you have a choice of seats, take the one that puts the tough player on your right.

Watch how often the winner had position. Sit and observe for twenty hands without playing. Count how many big pots were won by the player acting last. The number surprises people.
