How a Hand of Poker Works

One hand, start to finish. This is the whole game.

Texas Hold'em is the version almost everyone plays, and it's the one this lesson covers. ClubWPT also spreads Omaha and Seven Card Stud, which follow different rules. Learn Hold'em first, then explore.

Here's exactly what happens in a hand, in order.

Before the cards: the blinds

Poker needs chips in the middle or nobody would ever play a hand. So two players are forced to bet before anyone sees a card. These forced bets are called blinds, because you're betting blind — you haven't seen your cards yet.

The small blind posts the smaller amount. The big blind posts the larger one, usually double. The big blind is the number everything else gets measured against, which is why players talk about stack sizes in big blinds rather than chips.

The dealer button is a marker that moves one seat to the left after every hand. It decides who posts the blinds and who acts when. Because it moves, everyone takes a turn in every seat.

Step 1: Your two cards

Everyone gets two cards face down. These are your hole cards, and only you can see them. They're yours for the whole hand.

Then the first round of betting happens. Starting to the left of the big blind, each player chooses one of these:

  • Fold — give up the hand, lose nothing more
  • Call — match the current bet to stay in
  • Raise — increase the bet, forcing others to match or fold

Step 2: The flop

Three cards go face up in the middle of the table. These are community cards — everyone shares them. Combined with your two hole cards, you're building the best five-card hand you can.

Another betting round. Now that there are cards on the table, you can also check, which means passing without betting. If everyone checks, play moves on for free.

Step 3: The turn

A fourth community card. Another betting round. Bets are usually larger here because the pot is bigger and there's less left to come.

Step 4: The river

The fifth and final community card. Last betting round. Everything is known now — no more cards are coming, so whatever you have is what you have.

Step 5: Showdown

If two or more players are still in after the final round, everyone shows their cards. Best five-card hand wins the pot.

You make that hand from any combination of your two hole cards and the five community cards. Sometimes your best hand uses both of your cards, sometimes one, sometimes neither.

The part people miss

Most hands never reach showdown. Somebody bets, everybody else folds, and the pot gets pushed without anyone showing anything.

That's not a technicality. It's the whole game. You don't need the best hand to win a pot. You need everyone else to decide theirs isn't worth continuing with. Understanding that is the difference between playing your cards and playing poker.
